# ALMOST — Every Unfinished Thing Has a Story

**Authorized extension — room music:** The learner supplies three local MP3 tracks and requests one per room with a clickable animated SVG control. `room-music.md` defines opt-in playback; it supersedes the earlier no-audio boundary only for optional room music. Exhibit reveal/light effects remain silent and independent.

**Authorized extension — Observatory universe:** The learner requests one star per exhibit, visitor-chosen golden connections, and optional names for each connected group. `observatory-universe.md` defines this independent local star map. Connections and naming never alter exhibit words or lasting lights; no AI supplies relationships or meanings.

**Authorized extension — Conservatory journal:** The learner requests personal memory recording with dated/timed entries. `journal.md` defines an independent browser journal with explicit saves, original recording metadata, edit timestamps and safe draft/retry recovery. This adds journal-only persistence; exhibit records, creation drafts and other room discoveries retain their existing behavior.

**Authorized extension — two story rooms:** The learner explicitly requests two differently themed rooms with their own stories and features. `story-rooms.md` supersedes the prior single-room limit for this extension. The Main Hall keeps the unchanged personal collection; the new rooms are optional, lightweight, deterministic experiences with no new persistence or services.

A small, atmospheric virtual museum where an unfinished creative idea becomes a personal exhibit worth remembering.

## The Unique Kernel

“The museum transforms how their idea is presented, not what their idea means.” The visitor's words become a museum plaque beside a handcrafted artifact. Touching the artifact briefly brings it alive: a way to “leave a little light on” without completing, improving, or committing to revive the project. Its light never fades because of absence.

## Who It's For

A visitor with an unfinished creative project, hobby, or idea they want to remember without being asked to finish it. They may feel fondness, embarrassment, pain, or simple curiosity; the museum does not assume which. A judge should be able to try that experience immediately, without an account or setup. Existing ways these visitors preserve their unfinished work have not been established.

## The Core Loop

Enter the museum → describe an unfinished idea → review and edit its exhibit title and description → reveal its artifact and plaque in the museum → touch the artifact to briefly bring it alive → leave and return to the saved collection when desired.

Returning is an invitation to remember, never an obligation or engagement goal.

## Inspiration & Identity

The learner’s later hands-on review authorizes a larger architectural presentation of this same hall: window alcove, collection wall and foreground curator desk, with optional ephemeral environmental discoveries. See `hall-redesign.md`. The hall’s distant arch remains scenery; the two destinations in `story-rooms.md` are reached through named signposts. No new services.

An intimate place between a dream and a memory. Updated by the learner's authorized Midnight Keepsake Museum revision: a single asymmetric storybook diorama, midnight blue and soft forest green, butter-yellow lanterns, dusty peach/lavender, tactile cream labels and wooden shelves. Pip, an original sleepy curator cat, quietly accompanies creation and remembering. No direct imitation of an existing artist or work. See `redesign.md` for the revision boundary.

“The museum preserves beginnings, not endings.” Aim for quiet wonder and recognition, leaving visitors free to decide how they feel. Avoid a memorial atmosphere, forced positivity, therapeutic language, or invented emotional interpretations.

## Why This Matters to the Learner

“Something doesn't have to be completed to have meant something.” The imagination and effort behind a beginning were real. The learner wants to practice creative product design, visual storytelling, and thoughtful interactions through a cohesive experience grounded in their own vision.

## What "Working" Looks Like

A judge opens a live link, enters an atmospheric museum, describes an unfinished idea, edits a suggested title and their description, and creates an exhibit. Their words appear on an elegant plaque as a handcrafted artifact arrives and its light gently turns on. Touching it produces a brief, intentional animation. Reloading or revisiting in the same browser retains the exhibit. The central journey should be demonstrable in about a minute; this is a proposed demo target, not a measured result.

The memorable beat is an ordinary unfinished idea receiving a beautiful place in the museum, then briefly coming alive when remembered.

## The POC Boundary

- Three lightweight museum settings: the Hall of Beginnings holds the personal collection; Moonseed Conservatory and Pocket-Sky Observatory offer distinct curator stories and optional deterministic discoveries. Keep the small handcrafted artifact set.
- One complete creation-and-revisit loop. The visitor supplies a short description; simple, transparent templates suggest a title. Both title and description remain editable. No rewriting that invents feelings or meaning.
- Personalization through the visitor's words and a restrained set of artifact details such as color, lighting, or motion. Exact artifact selection and adaptation rules belong in the PRD.
- One polished “leave a little light on” interaction, expressed through brief artifact animation and light. Specific animations and any optional sound remain for the PRD.
- Exhibits saved on the same browser/device between visits. No account, backend, or cross-device promise; storage limitations must be explained appropriately in later product planning.
- A complete, usable end-to-end experience, with basic accessibility and testing included. Detailed states and behaviors belong in the PRD.
- ₹0 development and hosting target, with no mandatory paid AI APIs or runtime AI service. Aim for a free live deployment that judges can open immediately.
- Submission deadline: **October 27, 2026, at 2:30 AM IST (UTC+05:30)**. Total available active time: **6–10 hours**, including planning, implementation, testing, deployment, and demo recording. Finish the functional core early; protect remaining time for polish and submission preparation.
- Deliver a public GitHub repository and short demo video as required by the curriculum. Live deployment is strongly preferred; if hosting is blocked, preserve a working local demo and disclose the limitation.

## Later

- More artifact types, richer variations, and museum spaces beyond the approved three after the core experience proves itself.
- A dedicated peaceful-retirement experience from the initial concept; it is not necessary to demonstrate creation, remembering, and the light interaction.
- Further personalization beyond the small handcrafted system, only if it preserves visitor authorship and the project's cost constraints.

## Explicitly Cut

- Accounts, backend, public galleries, social sharing systems, and cross-device sync: unnecessary for the personal museum experiment and add setup and maintenance.
- Runtime AI text generation and per-submission generated artwork: unnecessary cost and reliability dependencies; presentation carries the magic.
- A large navigable museum, complex 3D world, and extensive artifact catalog: consume the time needed to finish and polish the core loop.
- Productivity tracking, completion goals, streaks, achievements, notifications, fading lights, and return pressure: conflict with ALMOST's emotional identity.
- Automated emotional interpretation and motivational advice: the visitor retains full control over meaning.
